Span and independence

What can we make? Is anything unnecessary?

About 20 minutesBook pp. 40–45 ↗
By the end

START SMALL

One idea for this lesson

Ask two questions: what can these vectors make, and is any vector unnecessary?

A linear combination just means “multiply each vector by a number, then add.”

How to read the symbols aloud

Read αu + βv as: “alpha times u, plus beta times v.” Alpha and beta are just names for numbers.

A span is everything your ingredients can build

A linear combination uses scalar weights: v = α₁v₁ + ⋯ + αₖvₖ. Its weights may be positive, negative, or zero. The span is the set of all results as those weights vary freely.

One nonzero vector spans a line through zero. Two nonparallel vectors in ℝ² span the plane. Two parallel vectors still span only a line. In ℝ³, two independent vectors span a plane, not all of ℝ³.

Independence is a test for redundant ingredients

α₁v₁ + ⋯ + αₖvₖ = 0   ⇒   α₁ = ⋯ = αₖ = 0

If this implication holds, the vectors are independent. If any nonzero list of coefficients produces zero, they are dependent. “Nontrivial” means at least one coefficient is nonzero, not that every coefficient must be nonzero.

Why test against zero? If α₁v₁ + ⋯ + αₖvₖ = 0 and αⱼ ≠ 0, rearrange to express vⱼ using the others. It adds no new reach. Conversely, any such redundant expression can be moved to one side to produce a nontrivial zero combination.

A small example that prevents a big misconception

Take u = (1,0), v = (0,1), and w = (1,1). No pair is parallel, but u + v − w = 0. The three vectors are dependent.

Pairwise nonparallel is not enough

For two nonzero vectors, dependence means one is a scalar multiple of the other. With three or more vectors, dependence may involve several vectors at once. Any set containing zero is dependent, since 1·0 already supplies a nontrivial relation.

Put the vectors into the columns of A and solve Aα = 0. A pivot in every column means independence. A free variable means dependence. You only need REF to identify pivots.

Why a basis makes representations unique

Suppose the same output has two coefficient lists: ∑αᵢvᵢ = ∑βᵢvᵢ. Subtract to obtain ∑(αᵢ − βᵢ)vᵢ = 0. Independence forces every αᵢ − βᵢ to be zero. Therefore the lists are identical.

This argument explains why independence is about uniqueness, while spanning is about existence. A basis will need both.

SECOND READING · MORE DETAIL

Working with abstract vectors

Suppose b₁,…,bₖ are independent, and xⱼ = ∑ᵢ λᵢⱼbᵢ. You can test the xⱼ by testing their coordinate columns λⱼ. A zero combination of the xⱼ becomes a zero combination of the bᵢ; independence of the bᵢ forces the combined coordinates to vanish.

This is the method behind the book’s Example 2.15. It also means more than k linear combinations of k vectors must be dependent. In a two-dimensional space, a third vector cannot supply a third independent direction.

Pause & predict

Are p(t) = 1 + t and q(t) = 1 − t independent?

Give me a hint

Set αp + βq = 0 and compare the constant and t coefficients.

Show the reasoning

Yes. The constant term gives α + β = 0; the t coefficient gives α − β = 0. Hence α = β = 0. These two polynomials span all polynomials of degree at most 1.
