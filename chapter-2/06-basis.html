Basis, dimension, and rank

Count independent directions, not entries.

About 22 minutesBook pp. 44–48 ↗
By the end

IN THIS LESSON
START SMALL

One idea for this lesson

A basis gives enough directions, with no extra ones.

A line needs one direction. A plane needs two independent directions. That is the starting idea behind dimension.

How to read the symbols aloud

Read dim(U) = 2 as: “the dimension of U is two.”

English help · words used in this lesson

One basis; several bases. A basis spans the space and is independent.
dimension
The number of vectors in a basis.
rank
The number of independent columns, or the number of pivots.
full rank
The rank is as large as the matrix shape allows.

The equations have at least one solution.

First reading: just these three steps

  1. Explain why a line in 3D still has dimension one.
  2. Find pivot columns in the example.
  3. Take the matching columns from the original matrix.

You can stop after these steps and the “Pause & predict” question. Return to the formal details when the main idea feels familiar.

A basis is an exact set of ingredients

A basis of V is an independent set that spans V. Spanning guarantees that every vector has coordinates; independence guarantees that the coordinates are unique.

Equivalently, it is a minimal generating set: remove any vector and something becomes unreachable. It is also a maximal independent set within V: add another vector from V and redundancy appears.

The standard basis of ℝ³ is (1,0,0), (0,1,0), (0,0,1). But (1,0,0), (1,1,0), (1,1,1) is another basis. Basis vectors need not be perpendicular or have length 1.

Dimension counts directions

All bases of a finite-dimensional space have the same number of vectors. That number is its dimension. A line in ℝ³ has dimension 1 even though its vectors have three entries. The space of 2 × 3 matrices has dimension 6, because each of six entries can vary independently.

The space containing only zero has dimension 0 and the empty basis. If U ⊆ V are finite-dimensional subspaces, dim U ≤ dim V; equality forces U = V.

Extract a basis without losing the original space

A = 120121001   →   R = 120001000

Columns 1 and 3 contain pivots. Take columns 1 and 3 from the original A: (1,1,0) and (0,1,1). They form a basis of its column space; column 2 is twice column 1.

A common mistake in elimination

Row operations preserve dependence relations among columns, but generally move the column space itself. The reduced columns (1,0,0) and (0,1,0) are not a basis of the original column space here. Use the reduced matrix to find column indices; use A to retrieve the vectors.

Rank counts the useful directions

The rank r of an m × n matrix is the dimension of its column space. It also equals its row-space dimension and the number of pivots. Row rank and column rank are equal, although the row space lives in ℝⁿ and the column space lives in ℝᵐ.

rank(A) = rank(Aᵀ) ≤ min(m,n)

A basis for the row space can be read from the nonzero rows of REF. Alternatively, find pivot columns of Aᵀ and take the corresponding original rows of A.

“Full rank” means r = min(m,n). A tall full-rank matrix has independent columns. A wide full-rank matrix has independent rows. Only a square full-rank matrix has a two-sided inverse.

SECOND READING · MORE DETAIL

Predict solutions before finding them

Rank conditionConsequence for Ax = b
rank([A | b]) > rank(A)No solution: b supplies a direction outside the column span.
rank([A | b]) = rank(A) = nExactly one solution: consistent, with no free variables.
rank([A | b]) = rank(A) < nInfinitely many solutions: n − r free parameters.

For the A above, the output is (x₁ + 2x₂, x₁ + 2x₂ + x₃, x₃). Thus b must satisfy b₂ = b₁ + b₃. If it does, one free parameter remains. If it does not, elimination reveals a contradiction.

Pause & predict

A 4 × 6 matrix has rank 4. Is Ax = b solvable for every b ∈ ℝ⁴? Is the solution unique?

Give me a hint

The image has dimension 4. There are 6 input variables.

Show the reasoning

Every b is reachable, since the column space is all of ℝ⁴. Each system has two free parameters, so no solution is unique. Full row rank gives surjectivity, not injectivity.
