LESSON 00 / A gentle starting point

Small math steps. Simple English. No hurry.

About 15 minutesBook pp. 17–18 ↗
By the end

You can read a simple equation, work with negative numbers, and add or scale a two-number vector.

IN THIS LESSON

You can learn this in small pieces

You are learning two things at once: mathematics and the English used to describe it. A difficult sentence is not proof that the idea is too hard. First translate the sentence into simple words. Then try one small example.

For now, use a pencil and paper. Read for 10–15 minutes, then stop and try one calculation yourself. You do not need to remember every new word immediately. The lessons have an English help box and a first reading path. Sections marked second reading can wait.

1. A letter is a place for a number

In 2x + 1 = 7, the letter x stands for a number we want to find. The expression 2x means 2 × x. The multiplication sign is often left out.

2x + 1 = 7
2x = 6   (subtract 1 from both sides)
x = 3   (divide both sides by 2)

Check the answer: 2 × 3 + 1 = 7. The equal sign means “has the same value as.” When changing an equation, do the same thing to both sides.

2. Negative numbers need careful steps

A minus sign can mean a negative number, or an instruction to subtract. Parentheses show what belongs together.

3 − (−2) = 3 + 2 = 5
(−2) × 3 = −6
(−2) × (−3) = 6

In the last line, two negative factors give a positive product. Now try a calculation that will appear in row operations: 1 − 2(−1). First multiply: 2(−1) = −2. Then subtract: 1 − (−2) = 3. Do not skip that middle step.

3. A fraction is a number too

The fraction 1/2 means one divided by two. It is the same number as 0.5. Fractions can be useful because they keep calculations exact.

½ × 6 = 3
½ + ½ = 1
x/2 = 3   ⇒   x = 6

In this last line, the symbol ⇒ means “therefore” or “implies.” Multiply both sides by 2 to find x. You cannot divide by zero.

4. A vector is a list of numbers, for now

Start with the vector (2,1). It has two components: first 2, then 1. On a diagram, start at zero, move 2 units right and 1 unit up. This gives a useful picture of the vector.

The book usually writes the same numbers vertically:

(2,1)   written as a column:   21

Add matching components. Multiply each component when scaling:

(2,1) + (3,−1) = (5,0)
2(2,1) = (4,2)
−1(2,1) = (−2,−1)

A scalar is one number, such as 2. A vector has components. Later, the book makes “vector” more general. These small lists are enough to start.

5. Subscripts name a component

In x₁ and x₂, the small lowered numbers are subscripts. They mean “the first component” and “the second component.” They do not mean multiplication.

If x = (4,7), then x₁ = 4 and x₂ = 7. In contrast, x² is a raised 2 and usually means x times x when x is a scalar. Position matters.

Greek letters such as α (alpha), β (beta), and λ (lambda) are also just names. In αu + βv, α and β usually stand for scalar numbers.

6. Read a long expression in small pieces

You will see this kind of sentence:

x ∈ ℝ²

Read it as “x is a vector with two real-number components.” The symbol ∈ means “belongs to.” ℝ is the set of real numbers, including numbers such as −3, 0, 1/2, and √2. Here the 2 in ℝ² tells you the number of components; it is not asking you to square a number.

Now read A ∈ ℝ²ˣ³ as “A is a table of real numbers, with two rows and three columns.” You can replace the symbolic phrase with those words whenever you need to.

7. What does “show that” ask you to do?

“Calculate” asks you to find a value. “Show that” or “prove” asks you to explain why a statement must be true. Trying a few examples may help you understand, but it does not prove a statement about every possible input.

To show a universal claim is false, however, one failing example is enough. The claim “every vector in ℝ² has a positive first component” is false because (−1,2) does not. Such an example is called a counterexample.

“If P, then Q” says: whenever P holds, Q follows. It does not automatically say that Q implies P. “If and only if” asserts both directions.

Pause & predict

Try three small checks: solve 3x + 2 = 11; calculate (1,−2) + (3,5); calculate 2(1,−2).

Give me a hint

For the equation, subtract 2 on both sides first. For the vectors, work on matching components.

Show the reasoning

The answers are x = 3, (4,3), and (2,−4). If any step is unclear, reread only that part above. These are the basic actions used in the first lesson.
